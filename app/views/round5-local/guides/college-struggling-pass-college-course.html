I'm struggling to pass my college course

If you're struggling with your college course and worried you may not pass, there are people who can help you decide your next steps.

Who to speak to and when

Course tutor

You should speak to your tutor as soon as possible. They'll be able to offer advice on specific things you could do to help.

Any teacher or member of staff

If you prefer, it's okay to speak to any teacher or member of staff you feel comfortable with. For example, you might prefer to speak to your personal tutor first. They can listen to you and help you to create a plan.

Student support services

Your college will likely have a pastoral officer as part of their student support services if you'd rather talk to them about how you're feeling. They might be called something different at your college. If you're not sure, you can ask a teacher.

Learning support or SEND department

You might feel that you're finding things difficult because of a learning difficulty, neurodivergence or disability. You can speak to a staff member who works in the learning support or special educational needs and disabilities (SEND) department. If you're not sure who this might be, you can ask your tutor for advice.

Careers adviser

A careers adviser can help you understand how different options might affect your future plans. They can also help you think through alternative routes if you're worried about passing your course.

Think about why you're struggling

There are many reasons why you might be struggling with one or more of your courses in college.

It could be that you found the subject easier during GCSEs, but it seems to be different or more difficult at college.

This is completely normal and could be due to:

  • the course covering different topics than it did at GCSE
  • the subject is different or harder than you expected
  • new tutors in college, or different teaching styles
  • struggling with essays or exams – you may find coursework more suited to the way you learn, for example
  • something in your personal life that is affecting your studies

What you can do next

Depending on what stage you're at in college, not passing your course can mean different things. It's normal to feel stressed, but there are things you can do that can help.

You may be able to:

  • change your subject or course if it's still early in the academic year
  • drop a course or subject if you're doing more than one so you can reduce your timetable and focus on the courses you feel more confident in
  • swap to another class if there's more than one class of this subject
  • resit anything you did not pass
  • get feedback on assessments you did not pass, so you can focus on what to improve

Your teacher can help you decide what option might suit you best. Think about what's important to you now and for your next step. For example, if you need certain grades or qualifications for further study or job applications. This will help you narrow down what to do.

What you can ask

Whoever you choose to talk to, it's a good step towards changing things for the better.

It can be hard knowing how to get the most out of conversations or even how to start them.

Here are a few prompts of what to say to a tutor or teacher if you're struggling to pass your course at college:

“I am struggling to pass this course. Are you the best person to speak to about that, or is there someone else I should speak to in college?”

“Can you help me work out what to do or what will happen if I want to drop this course?”

“What are my options if I don't pass my resits?”

“Is it possible for me to drop or change one of my courses or subjects?”
