I’m stuck on a course I do not like

If your course is not what you hoped for, you’re not alone. Even if you cannot change now, it can still help you build skills for your next step.

If your course is not what you expected

It’s common to start a course and find it’s not quite what you expected. It can feel like dropping out is easier than continuing. It's okay if your path is not straightforward.

Who to speak to in college

Speak to someone when you feel ready to talk about it. You do not need to speak to everyone – start by choosing one person you feel most comfortable with.

Subject teacher

Speak to your subject teacher if you're struggling with the work, not sure why the course matters or want to know what topics are coming next.

They may be able to explain how the course links to jobs, apprenticeships, training or further study. They can also help you get extra support.

A teacher or member of staff

If you prefer, it’s okay to speak to any teacher or member of staff you feel comfortable with. For example, you might prefer to speak to your personal tutor first. They’ll listen and can help you find out who the right person to speak to is.

Student support services

Your college will likely have a pastoral officer as part of their student support services who you can talk to about how you’re feeling.

They can help if your course is affecting your mood, confidence, attendance or motivation.

Careers adviser

A careers adviser can help you understand what your current course can lead to and explore your options for the future.

They can also help you plan steps towards the career, course, apprenticeship or training you want.

Find advice for Norfolk and Suffolk

There are organisations in Norfolk and Suffolk that can help you if you’re stuck or unsure what to do next.

YES (Youth employment service)

East Suffolk

Support you can get

You can get a free coach to help you with your next step. They can support you with anything from figuring out your next step to feeling more confident with job applications. They’ll be there to chat to so you’re never figuring it out on your own.

Who can get help

You need to be aged 16 to 24 and live in East Suffolk.

What to expect

In your first session, you’ll chat about what you might need and make sure you’re eligible. Your first call will be over the phone or online.

How to contact

You can book a session with a coach. You can choose a date and time that works for you.

Help You Choose

Norfolk

Support you can get

Help you Choose is designed to help you plan what to do next as a young person in Norfolk.

Where to start

You can browse available courses that start throughout the year. You can also find out about local career events, get help with decisions and see advice for finding a job.

The Source

Suffolk

Support you can get

The Source is run by Suffolk County Council for young people who are feeling stuck.

Where to start

It can help you find support if you’re feeling stuck or unsure what to do next with things like college, jobs, your career or wellbeing. You can see what services in Suffolk are available and find out about local events or job fairs.

Volunteer Suffolk

East Suffolk

Support you can get

Volunteer Suffolk helps you find volunteering opportunities across Suffolk. You can search for opportunities near you to build your skills and confidence. New volunteering positions are added each week so it’s worth checking back if you do not spot anything you’re interested.

What age you can be

There are volunteer positions for all ages. Check the page of roles you’re interested into check if there is a minimum age.

How to contact

Volunteer Suffolk also have a youth volunteering programme for young people aged 14 to 25. You can contact them if you’re interested in joining.

I can be a

Norfolk and Suffolk

Support you can get

I can be a is a website for young people in Norfolk and Suffolk up to the age of 25.

Where to start

There are lots of tools and information to explore. Here are some of the main features.

You can:

  • discover job industries that are popular in the area and learn about local employers
  • browse a directory of employers
  • find local job opportunities that may not appear on larger job sites like Indeed

Things you can gain from any course

Staying on your course can still be worthwhile, even if it does not feel useful right now.

You may gain:

  • a qualification that might help you apply for another course, apprenticeship, training or work
  • experience dealing with a difficult situation and asking for support when you need it
  • confidence from seeing the progress you make, even if the course is not your long-term plan
  • practical or technical skills you can use beyond college
  • skills like communication, organisation, problem-solving, teamwork and time management

It may help to think of this course as one part of your plan, not your whole future.

How to keep going when motivation is low

Motivation can be harder when you are studying something you do not enjoy. Try to focus on small things you can do.

You could try:

  • setting one small goal, like attending the next lesson
  • using a routine that works for you, for example setting a regular time to do coursework
  • making time for things you enjoy
  • linking assignments or projects to something you're interested in if possible
  • speaking to someone if it's affecting your mood, attendance or ability to complete work

If you have time and it's manageable, you could look for volunteering, an online course, or a club linked to what you want to do next.

What you can ask

It’s okay to feel nervous to say how you feel. You do not have to explain everything at once. Start by saying that you're not enjoying the course and would like help thinking about what to do next.

If you are not sure how to start the conversation, you could say:

“I’m not enjoying this course. Can we talk about what I can do?”

“I’m not sure this course is right for me. Can you help me understand my options?”

“Is there any extra support I can get while I finish this course?”

“Who else should I speak to about making a plan?”

You may not be able to change course immediately, but you can still make choices and have some control over what happens next.
