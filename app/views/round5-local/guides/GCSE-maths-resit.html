I need to pass my GCSE maths resit

It can be tough having to keep resitting your maths GCSE. You're not the only one. Over 200,000 young people in England sat their maths GCSE again in 2025.

Understand what you're struggling with

Have a think about what part of the resit is stressing you out. You might find it easier to ask for help about something specific.

Think about:

  • whether you're missing out on passing by only a few marks
  • if your nerves are taking over on exam day
  • if you're struggling to answer questions on specific topics

If you want to see your completed exam paper, contact your school or college as soon as possible after receiving your results. There may be deadlines and fees to do this.

Whether or not you review your paper, your maths teacher can help you understand which topics to focus on before you resit.

Who to speak to and when

Your GCSE maths teacher

You should let your maths teacher know as soon as possible that you want help. They can create a plan with you about what to focus on for your resit.

Any teacher or member of staff

If you prefer, it's okay to speak to any teacher or member of staff you feel comfortable with. They can listen and help you to create a plan.

Student support services

Your college or sixth form will likely have a pastoral or support team you can speak to for help with your resit. They can also help if your resit is affecting your confidence, motivation or wellbeing.

Maths tutor

You could get tutoring from someone familiar with your exam paper. Your teacher might know older students who have recently passed the exam who can help. Or, if you're able to pay, they might be able to recommend a local or online tutor.

Alternative to GCSE maths

If you're struggling to pass after several resits, you might be able to do a Functional Skills level 2 qualification instead. This is equivalent to a GCSE grade 4 pass and is often accepted as an alternative to a GCSE.

Your maths teacher or student support services can help see if you're eligible to do a functional skills course. You can find out more functional skills qualifications from Prospects.

You might plan to start another course, apprenticeship or training after college or sixth form. If so, a careers adviser can help you find out if a functional skills qualification would be accepted instead of a GCSE.

Understand your options after college or sixth form

Your maths teacher or student support services can help you understand what your options are if you do or do not pass maths.

It's normal to be worried and want a plan. There are options if you need to do your maths resit after college. GCSE maths courses are often available in libraries, adult education centres or other local venues.

You might want to speak to a careers adviser so you can chat about what you want to do. They can help you create a plan to get there.

What you can ask

Here are a few prompts of what to say to get help with your maths resit:

“Are there any resources I can use that are different from what I've already tried?”

“Can I get any further help or tutoring to help me pass?”

“Can you show me why my answer to this question did not work?”
