I want to create a CV or make my CV better

If you’re struggling to know what to include in your CV, you’re not alone. Find out who can help you and see examples CVs from other young people.

Who can help in Norfolk and Suffolk

Sometimes it can be confusing to know where to start. There’s no right or wrong answer. There are often CV drop-in sessions in places like libraries, community centres and youth hubs.

Here are some organisations that can help in Norfolk and Suffolk.

Speak to a careers adviser

England

Support you can get

You can speak to a careers adviser on the phone. They can help with your CV, give you job or career advice and help you work out what to do next.

Who can get help

If you’re not in school, training or are 18 or older, you can speak to a careers adviser through the Jobs and Careers Service. You can also see who you can speak to depending on your situation.

How to contact

You can speak to a careers adviser for free on 0800 072 088.

BOOST

West Norfolk and Breckland

Support you can get

You can get personalised help and ongoing support. This might be in person or online. Activities include: 121 mentoring, CV skills, mock interviews and work experience opportunities.

Who can get help

You can get support if you’re aged 16 to 24, live in West Norfolk or Breckland and are not in education, employment or training.

What to expect

You’ll first meet with a member of the BOOST team. This will be a friendly chat so they can understand more about you and what you’re interested in.

How to contact

  • Self refer using the online form for BOOST
  • For West Norfolk, email: boost@norfolk.gov.uk
  • For Breckland, email: boostbreckland@norfolk.gov.uk

Help You Choose

Norfolk

Support you can get

Help You Choose is designed to help you plan what to do next as a young person in Norfolk. You might also be able to speak to an adviser. They’ll listen to you and help you take a next step with things like choosing a new course, thinking about your career or finding a job.

Who can get help

You can speak to an adviser if you live in Norfolk and are not in education, training or work. You need to be aged 16 to 19, or up to 25 if you have an EHCP.

What to expect

You’ll be able to talk to an adviser about what you’re worried about and what you might need help with. If you send an email or message, you should expect to hear back within 1 working day.

How to contact

You can contact them by email, phone or online. View contact details for Help You Choose.

The Source

Suffolk

Support you can get

The Source is run by Suffolk County Council for young people who are feeling stuck. You might be able to get help from an adviser. They can chat to you if you’re not sure what to do next with things like college, jobs or your career. You can also find out about local events or job fairs.

Who can get help

You can speak to a member of the youth services support team if you live in Suffolk and are not in further education, training or work. You need to be aged 16 to 18, or up to 25 if you have an EHCP.

What to expect

You’ll be able to talk to an adviser about what you’re worried about and what you might need help with. They might reach out by phone or email.

How to contact

You can contact them by email, phone or online form. View contact details for the Suffolk Youth Support Services team.

YES (Youth employment service)

East Suffolk

Support you can get

You can get a free coach to help you with your next step. They can support you with anything from figuring out what to do next to feeling more confident with job applications. They’ll be there to chat to so you’re never figuring it out on your own.

Who can get help

You need to be aged 16 to 24 and live in East Suffolk.

What to expect

In your first session, you’ll chat about what you might need and make sure you’re eligible. Your first call will be over the phone or online.

How to contact

You can book a session with a coach. You can choose a date and time that works for you.

I can be a

Norfolk and Suffolk

Support you can get

I can be a is a website for young people in Norfolk and Suffolk up to the age of 25.

Where to start

There are lots of tools and information to explore. Here are some of the main features.

You can:

  • discover job industries that are popular in the area and learn about local employers
  • browse a directory of employers
  • find local job opportunities that may not appear on larger job sites like Indeed

Create a few tailored CVs for different types of jobs

We know tailoring your CV for every job application can feel exhausting.

If you have the energy to do it for every single one, that’s great. However, it’s common to lose motivation, especially if you’re been applying for a while.

We recommend you have a few tailored CVs for the types of jobs you’re applying for. You can make sure each CV emphasises the skills and experience needed for that type of role. This might help make it feel more manageable going forward.

For example, you might have a CV tailored towards:

  • working in a pub, restaurant or cafe
  • seasonal retail jobs in shops or supermarkets like Tesco
  • babysitting and nursery work experience

You can see if a friend or family member can read your tailored CV. It’s useful to get a fresh perspective. They might spot things you had not thought about, and give you some ideas of what to change.

Online resources and example CVs

You might want to feel like you’re making progress now. Here are some articles and tools we know other young people have found useful.

You can:

  • see an example CV from a 16 year old who does not have much work experience from Prospects
  • find out what to include if this is your first CV from Youth Employment UK
  • get a professional person to review your CV for free from Young Women’s Trust
  • build a CV for free on GOV.UK if you’re not sure where to start
  • ask for feedback on your CV on a forum like Reddit, r/UKjobs is a useful subreddit

What you can ask

Here are a few prompts of what to say to a careers adviser or work coach about your CV:

“I have a CV but want to see if what I’m doing to tailor it works well or not”

“Do you have any example CVs you can show me of other people who don’t have lots of experience?”

“Can you help me understand what experience I can put on my CV?”
