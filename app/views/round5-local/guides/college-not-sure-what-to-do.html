I'm not sure what to do when I leave college

It’s normal to feel confused if you’re not sure what to do when you leave college. It's okay for your plans to change or for you to change your mind.

Think about why you're not sure what to do next

It’s hard to decide what to do after college. It can feel like a massive decision. You might find it useful to think of it as a choice for now. It’s not a permanent decision and you can change your mind if it does not feel right.

It can help to think about what’s going on in your life that’s making it harder to decide.

For example:

  • you had a plan, but you've changed your mind
  • you feel pressured to choose a specific course or go to uni
  • you're not sure you're going to get the grades you need
  • you're resitting GCSE maths or English and not sure how it might affect your next step

Understand what your options are

It can be quite daunting to think about what to do next when there are so many choices.

GOV.UK has advice about your options when you leave college at 18. Try to look at one option at a time and write down anything you’re not sure of. You can then take your questions to a teacher or careers adviser at college.

Or if you can, speak to a careers adviser at college when looking at the options. They can listen to how you feel, understand your situation and go through the options that best suit you.

Who to speak to in college

You’ll find it useful to talk to someone you trust. They’ll know your situation better and can help you work out what to do next.

Careers adviser

Your college will have a careers adviser you can speak to. They can help you look at your options for next year and what might suit you.

Student support services

Most colleges have a student support team who can listen to how you’re feeling. This might include pastoral officers and wellbeing or mental health support staff. They might be called something different at your college. If you’re not sure you can ask a teacher.

Any teacher or member of staff

It’s okay to speak to any teacher or member of staff you feel comfortable with. For example, you might prefer to speak to your personal tutor if you know them well.

Learning support or SEND department

You might feel that you’re finding it difficult to think about your next step because of a learning difficulty, neurodivergence or disability. You can speak to a staff member who works in the learning support or SEND department. If you’re not sure who this might be, you can ask your tutor for advice.

Find advice for Norfolk and Suffolk

There are organisations in Norfolk and Suffolk that can help you if you’re stuck or unsure what to do next.

Help You Choose

Norfolk

Support you can get

Help You Choose is designed to help you plan what to do next as a young person in Norfolk.

Where to start

You can browse available courses that start throughout the year. You can also find out about local career events, get help with decisions and see advice for finding a job.

The Source

Suffolk

Support you can get

The Source is run by Suffolk County Council for young people who are feeling stuck.

Where to start

It can help you find support if you’re feeling stuck or unsure what to do next with things like college, jobs, your career or wellbeing. You can see what services in Suffolk are available and find out about local events or job fairs.

Volunteer Suffolk

Suffolk

Support you can get

Volunteer Suffolk helps you find volunteering opportunities across Suffolk. You can search for opportunities near you to build your skills and confidence. New volunteering positions are added each week so it’s worth checking back if you do not spot anything you’re interested.

What age you can be

There are volunteer positions for all ages. Check the page of roles you’re interested into check if there is a minimum age.

How to contact

Volunteer Suffolk also have a youth volunteering programme for young people aged 14 to 25. You can contact them if you’re interested in joining.

I can be a

Norfolk and Suffolk

Support you can get

I can be a is a website for young people in Norfolk and Suffolk up to the age of 25.

Where to start

There are lots of tools and information to explore. Here are some of the main features.

You can:

  • discover job industries that are popular in the area and learn about local employers
  • browse a directory of employers
  • find local job opportunities that may not appear on larger job sites like Indeed

YES (Youth employment service)

East Suffolk

Support you can get

You can get a free coach to help you with your next step. They can support you with anything from figuring out what to do next to feeling more confident with job applications. They’ll be there to chat to so you’re never figuring it out on your own.

Who can get help

You need to be aged 16 to 24 and live in East Suffolk.

What to expect

In your first session, you’ll chat about what you might need and make sure you’re eligible. Your first call will be over the phone or online.

How to contact

You can book a session with a coach. You can choose a date and time that works for you.

What to ask

It can be hard to know how to take the first step of asking for help.

Here are a few prompts of what to say to a tutor or teacher if you’re struggling to decide what to do after college:

“Can I talk to you about my plan for after college?”

“I don’t want to go to uni. What other options are there I can do?”

“What are my options if I've not passed GCSE maths?”

“Are there any organisations that can help me after I leave college?”
