I never hear back from applications

We know it’s tough to keep applying for jobs and apprenticeships if you’re not hearing anything back. It can be hard to keep motivated if you feel like you’re going in circles.

What you can try next

If you’ve asked for feedback from employers and have been ignored, you’re not alone. It can be demotivating to not understand why your application was rejected. It can make it difficult to know what to change.

Here are some things to try if you’re feeling stuck. If you’ve already tried some or all of these, that’s okay. It can be unclear why one application succeeds while another does not. Try not to be too hard on yourself. Sometimes the best thing to do is to pause and come back with a fresher head tomorrow.

Today you could:

  • search for local organisations near you using the form on this page
  • see if a friend or family member can look at your most recent job application
  • ask for feedback on your CV or cover letter on a forum like Reddit, r/UKjobs is a useful subreddit
  • look back at one application and see if you would change anything
  • set one small goal for tomorrow rather than thinking of everything all at once
  • take a break from applications and make time for things you enjoy

How to ask for feedback from an interview

If you had an interview, an employer should take the time to give you feedback after the interview. We know this does not always happen, even when you ask for it. If you need help asking for feedback, you can see how to write an email from Youth Employment UK.

Online resources for help with job applications

Here are some articles and tools we know other young people have found useful.

You can:

  • see an example CV from a 16 year old who does not have much work experience from Prospects
  • use the AI work assistant from GOV.UK to get feedback on a job application. You’ll need an email address and a phone number for this.
  • find out what to include if this is your first CV from Youth Employment UK

What you can ask

If you need to send an email, we know this can feel daunting. You can get a template for how to write an email from Youth Employment UK.

Here are a few prompts of what to say to a friend, careers adviser or work coach if you need help:

“I’ve applied for quite a few jobs but I’m not hearing back. Can you look at one of my applications and see what you think?”

“I’m not sure if it’s my CV, my application or the jobs I’m applying for. Can you help me work out what to try next?”

“Can you read my application and tell me if you think it meets what they're looking for in the job advert?”
