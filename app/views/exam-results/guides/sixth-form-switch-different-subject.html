I want to switch subjects at sixth form

It takes a lot to realise one of your subjects is not right for you. If you need to make a change, it's okay to talk about it.

Who to speak to and when

Subject teacher

You should speak to your subject teacher as soon as possible. They can help you understand your options or tell you who the right person to speak to is. 

Any teacher or member of staff

If you prefer, it’s okay to speak to any teacher or member of staff you feel comfortable with. For example, you might prefer to speak to your personal tutor first. They’ll listen and can help you find out who the right person to speak to is.  

Pastoral team 

You’ll have a pastoral team at your school you could talk to about how you’re feeling. The team may include learning mentors, pastoral officers or wellbeing staff. They might be called something different at your sixth form. If you’re not sure, you can ask a teacher.

Think about why you want to change subjects

Your teacher will want to understand why you want to switch subjects. They’ll need to know if you’re facing any particular issues.

Think about if:

  • the subject is different from what you thought it would be
  • it's a bigger step up in difficulty than you expected
  • you're struggling with essays or exams
  • you're not enjoying the class or teaching 
  • something in your personal life is affecting how you feel

This can help you see if there’s anything you could try before making a final decision. There might not be and that’s okay, but it’s worth thinking about.

For example, you could find out what topics are coming up soon to see if they interest you. You might also be able to get support with study skills such as essay writing, as well as help with personal issues. 

How switching subjects works in sixth form

If you know you want to switch, your teacher might need you to put your request in writing.

You may be able to switch up to October half term, depending on the subject and qualification. After this, it might be difficult to catch up on missed work.

Your teacher will check if: 

  • you can switch or if it’s too late in the year 
  • you meet the entry requirements for the subject
  • the subject class you want to do has space
  • switching would cause any clashes with your other lessons

You might be able to drop an A Level subject if that's right for you. Your teacher or careers adviser will help see if this will impact your next steps after sixth form.

If you do switch subjects, you might have a meeting about it. A parent, carer or family member can come with you if you’d find that helpful - you get to decide.

What to do if it’s too late in the year to switch

It’s okay to be disappointed if it’s too late in the year to switch. Your teacher will explain your options so you can create a plan.

You might be able to change subjects in September. This might mean staying on an extra year. Your teacher can explain if this is an option in your sixth form.

It can be hard to stay motivated if you’re not enjoying one of your subjects. Something that might help is speaking to a careers adviser at your sixth form. Ask a teacher to see when you can speak to them if you’re not sure.

A careers adviser can help you:

  • look again at your subject choices and what they can lead to
  • think about what subject or career might be a better fit for you
  • find out about work experience or volunteering in the subject or career you're interested in
  • see how what you're learning on your course might still be useful

What you can ask

It can be hard to know how to take the first step of asking for help.  Here are a few prompts of what to say to a teacher about wanting to change subjects: 

“Can you explain to me how switching subjects works?” 

“I want to change subjects. Are you the best person to speak to about that, or is there someone else I should speak to in sixth form?” 

“I'm not enjoying this subject but it’s too late to switch. What can I do?” 

“How do I make an appointment with a careers adviser?” 

Talk to someone about your mental health

If you need help urgently, you do not have to deal with it by yourself.

There are people who can help you.
