I want to switch to a different course at college

It takes a lot to realise you need to change direction. Whether you've started a course you did not want to do, or have changed your mind, it's okay to talk about it.

Who to speak to and when

Course teacher

You should speak to your course teacher as soon as possible. They will tell you how to switch course and how the process works in your college.

Any teacher or member of staff

If you prefer, it's okay to speak to any teacher or member of staff you feel comfortable with. For example, you might prefer to speak to your personal tutor first. They'll listen and can help you find out who the right person to speak to is.

Student support services

Your college will likely have a pastoral officer as part of their student support services if you'd rather talk to them about how you're feeling. They might be called something different at your college. If you're not sure, you can ask a teacher.

Think about why you want to change course

Your teacher will want to understand why you want to change course. They’ll need to know if there are any specific issues you’re facing.

Think about if:

  • the subject is different from what you thought it would be
  • it's a bigger step up in difficulty than you expected
  • you're struggling with essays or exams
  • you're not enjoying the class or teaching
  • something in your personal life is affecting how you feel

Thinking about what you're not enjoying can help you see if there's anything you could try before making a final decision. There might not be and that's okay, but it's worth considering.

For example, you could find out what topics are coming up soon to see if they interest you. You might also be able to get support with study skills such as essay writing, as well as help with personal issues. 

How switching course works in college

If you know you want to switch, your teacher might need you to put your request in writing.

You may be able to switch course up to October half term, depending on the subject and qualification. After this, it might be difficult to catch up on missed work.

Your teacher will check if: 

  • you can switch or if it’s too late in the year 
  • you meet the entry requirements of the course 
  • the course you want to do has space or if you can be added to a waiting list  
  • switching would cause any clashes with your other lessons 
  • there's space on a course at a different college 

If you’re doing A levels, you might be able to switch to another subject. Or, you might be able to drop a subject if that’s right for you. Your teacher will help see if this will impact your next step after college.

If you do switch courses, you might have a meeting about it. A parent, carer or family member can come with you if you’d find that helpful – you get to decide. 

What to do if it’s too late in the year to switch

It’s okay to be disappointed if it’s too late in the year to switch. Your teacher will explain your options so you can create a plan.

You can ask if you can enrol onto a new course next September. If you'll be 19 or over when starting a new course, it's a good idea to check your funding options early. There may be different types of support available. 

In the meantime, you might want to speak to your college careers adviser. They can help you:

  • explore your options and think about what course or career might be a better fit for you 
  • find out about work experience or volunteering in the subject you’re interested in
  • see how

What you can ask

Here are a few prompts of what to say to a teacher about wanting to change course:

“Can you explain to me how switching courses works?”

“I want to change course. Are you the best person to speak to about that, or is there someone else I should speak to in college?”

“Can you help me work out what to do if I want to enrol on a new course next September?”

“How do I make an appointment with a careers adviser?”

Talk to someone about your mental health

If you need help urgently, you do not have to deal with it by yourself.

There are people who can help you.
