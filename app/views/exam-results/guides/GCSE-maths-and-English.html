I want to pass my GCSE maths and English resit

It can be tough having to keep resitting your maths and English GCSEs. You’re not the only one. Around 375,000 young people in England sat their maths or English GCSE again in 2025.

Understand what you’re struggling with

Have a think about what part of the resits are stressing you out. You might find it easier to ask for help about something specific.

Think about:

  • whether you're missing out on passing by only a few marks
  • if your nerves are taking over on exam day
  • if you’re struggling to answer questions on specific topics
  • if you're finding one of the papers more difficult

You can also review your last exam paper with your subject teacher. They can help you see what your strengths are and where to improve.

Who to speak to and when

We need a summary here

Your GCSE maths or English teachers

You should let your subject teacher know as soon as possible that you want help. They can create a plan with you about what to focus on for your resit.

Any teacher or member of staff

If you prefer, it’s okay to speak to any teacher or member of staff you feel comfortable with. They can listen and help you to create a plan.

Student support services

Your college or sixth form will likely have a pastoral officer or careers adviser you can speak to for help with your resit.

Maths or English tutor

You could get tutoring from someone that’s familiar with your exam paper. Your teacher might know older students who have recently passed who can help. Or, if you’re able to pay, they might be able to recommend a local or online tutor.

Alternative to GCSE English or maths

It can be quite daunting to think about what to do next where there are so many choices.

The National Careers Service has advice about your options when you leave college at 18. Try to look at one option at a time and write down anything you’re not sure of. You can then take your questions to a teacher or careers adviser at college.

Or if you can, speak to a careers adviser at college when looking at the options. They can listen to how you feel, understand your situation and go through the options that best suit you.

Understand your options after college or sixth form

We need a summary here

Your subject teacher or student support services can help you understand what your options are if you do or do not pass maths or English.

It’s normal to be worried and want a plan. There are options if you need to do a GCSE resit after college. GCSE maths and English courses are often available in libraries, adult education centres or other local venues.

You might want to speak to a careers adviser so you can chat about what you want to do. They can help you create a plan to get there.

You can also see advice if your plan has changed and you’re unsure what to do after college or sixth form.

Here are a few prompts of what to say to get help with your resits:

“Are there any resources I can use that are different from what I’ve already tried?”

“Can I get any further help or tutoring to help me pass?”

“Can you show me why my answer to this question did not work?”
