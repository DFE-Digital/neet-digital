I’m struggling to pass a subject in sixth form

You may find that you struggle with some subjects more than others. There are people who can help you.

Who to speak to and when

Subject teacher

You should speak to your subject teacher as soon as possible. They'll be able to offer advice on specific things you could do to help.

Pastoral team 

You’ll have a pastoral team at your school who you can talk to about how you’re feeling. The team may include learning mentors, pastoral officers or wellbeing staff. They might be called something different at your sixth form. If you’re not sure, you can ask a teacher. 

Any teacher or member of staff

If you prefer, it’s okay to speak to any teacher or member of staff you feel comfortable with. For example, you might prefer to speak to your personal tutor first. They can listen to you and help you to create a plan.  

Learning support or SEND department 

You might feel that you’re finding it difficult to think about your next step because of a learning difficulty, neurodivergence or disability. You can speak to a staff member who works in the learning support or SEND department. If you’re not sure who this might be, you can ask your tutor for advice.

Careers adviser

A careers adviser can help you understand how different options might affect your future plans. They can also help you think through alternative routes if you're worried about passing your course.

Think about why you’re struggling

There are many reasons why you might be struggling with one or more subjects in sixth form. 

It could be that you found the subject easier during GCSEs, but it seems to be different or more difficult now.   

This is completely normal and can be due to: 

  • the course covering different topics than it did at GCSE 
  • the subject is different or harder than you expected 
  • a different teacher to your previous teacher who might have a different teaching style 
  • struggling with essays or exams - you may find coursework more suited to the way you learn, for example 
  • something in your personal life that is affecting your studies

What you can do next

Year 12 options

If you’re in year 12, you might be able to:

  • change your subject if it’s still early in the academic year 
  • drop a subject and so you can reduce your timetable and focus on the subjects you feel more confident in
  • swap to another class if there is more than one class of this subject
  • resit anything you did not pass
  • take up a new course in year 13 – some sixth forms offer courses that can be done over one year
  • get feedback on assessments you did not pass, so you can focus on what to improve

Your teacher can help you decide which option best suits your situation.

Year 13 options

If you’re in year 13, your options may be slightly different. You could:

  • drop one or more courses to reduce your timetable and focus on the courses you feel more confident to pass
  • discuss your resit options, working out how much difference it will make to your overall grades

Think about what's important to you now and for your next step. For example, if you need certain grades or qualifications for further study or job applications. This will help you narrow down what to do.

What you can ask

Whoever you choose to talk to, it’s a good step towards changing things for the better.

It can be hard knowing how to get the most out of conversations or even how to start them.

Here are a few prompts of what to say to a tutor or teacher if you’re struggling to pass a subject in sixth form:

“I am struggling to pass this subject. Are you the best person to speak to about that, or is there someone else I should speak to in sixth form?”

“Can you help me work out what to do or what will happen if I want to drop this subject?”

“What are my options if I don’t pass my resits?”

“Is it possible for me to drop or change one of my subjects?”

Talk to someone about your mental health

If you need help urgently, you do not have to deal with it by yourself.

There are people who can help you.
