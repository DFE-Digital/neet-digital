I want to pass my GCSE English resit

It can be tough having to keep resitting your English GCSE. You’re not the only one. Over 175,000 young people in England sat their English GCSE again in 2025.

Text or email this page

Understand what you’re struggling with

Have a think about what part of the resit is stressing you out. You might find it easier to ask for help about something specific.

Think about:

  • whether you're missing out on passing by only a few marks
  • if your nerves are taking over on exam day
  • if you're finding one of the papers more difficult

You can also review your last exam paper with your English teacher. They can help you see what your strengths are and where to improve.

Who to speak to and when

Your GCSE English teacher
You should let your English teacher know as soon as possible that you want help. They can create a plan with you about what to focus on for your resit.

Any teacher or member of staff
If you prefer, it’s okay to speak to any teacher or member of staff you feel comfortable with. They can listen and help you to create a plan.

Student support services
Your college or sixth form will likely have a pastoral officer or careers adviser you can speak to for help with your resit.

English tutor
You could get tutoring from someone that’s familiar with your exam paper. Your teacher might know older students who have recently passed who can help. Or, if you’re able to pay, they might be able to recommend a local or online tutor.

Alternative to GCSE English

If you’re struggling to pass after several resits, you might be able to do a Functional Skills level 2 qualification instead. This is equivalent to a GCSE grade 4 pass and is widely accepted as an alternative to a GCSE.

Your English teacher or student support services can help see if you’re eligible to do a functional skills course. You can find out more functional skills qualifications from Prospects.

You might plan to start another course after college or sixth form. If so, a careers adviser can help you find out if your course accepts functional skills qualifications.

Understand your options after college or sixth form

Your English teacher or student support services can help you understand what your options are if you do or do not pass English.

It’s normal to be worried and want a plan. There are options if you need to do your English resit after college. GCSE English courses are often available in libraries, adult education centres or other local venues.

You might want to speak to a careers adviser so you can chat about what you want to do. They can help you create a plan to get there. 

You can also see advice if your plan has changed and you’re unsure what to do after college or sixth form.

What you can ask

Here are a few prompts of what to say to get help with your English resit:

“Are there any resources I can use that are different from what I’ve already tried?”

“Can I get any further help or tutoring to help me pass?”

“Can you show me why my answer to this question did not work?”

Talk to someone about your mental health

If you need help urgently, you do not have to deal with it by yourself.

There are people who can help you.
