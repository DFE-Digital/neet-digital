Who to speak to and when

Your GCSE maths teacher
You should let your maths teacher know as soon as possible that you want help. They can help create a plan of what to focus on for your next resit.

Any teacher If you don’t feel you can speak to your maths teacher, it’s okay to speak to any teacher you feel comfortable with. They should listen and help create a plan.

Student support services Your college or sixth form will likely have some form of pastoral officer or careers adviser you can speak to for help with your resit.

Maths tutor You could get tutoring from someone that’s familiar with your exam paper. Your teacher might know older students that have recently passed who can help. Or, if you’re able to pay, they should be able to recommend a local tutor.

Understand what you’re struggling with

It can be tough to resit GCSE maths and feel like you’re not getting anywhere. If that feels familiar, you’re not alone.

Have a think about what part of the resit is stressing you out. You might find it easier to ask for help about something specific. For example:

  • Are you missing out on passing by only a few marks?
  • Are your nerves taking over on exam day?
  • Are there specific topics you’re struggling to answer questions for?

You can also review your last exam paper with your maths teacher. They can help you see what your strengths are and where things went wrong.

Alternative to GCSE maths

If you’re struggling to pass after multiple tries, you might be able to do a Functional Skills level 2 qualification instead. This is equivalent to a grade 4 and is widely accepted as an alternative to a GCSE.

Your maths teacher or student support services can help see if you’re eligible. You can alsosee what different qualification levels mean on GOV.UK.

You might want to ask to speak to a careers adviser. They can help you see if your next step after college accepts a functional skills qualification.

Understand your options after college or sixth form

Your maths teacher or student support services should help you understand what your options are if you do or do not pass maths.

It’s normal to be worried and want a plan. There are options if you need to do your maths resit after college. GCSE maths courses are often available in libraries, adult education centres or other local venues.

You might want to speak to a careers adviser so you can chat about your goals. They can help you create a plan to get there.

You can also see advice if your plan has changed and you’re unsure what to do after college or sixth form.

What you can ask

Here are a few prompts of what to say to get help with your maths resit:

“Are there any resources I can use that are different from what I’ve already tried?”

“Can I get any further help or tutoring to help me pass?”

“Can you show me why my answer to this question didn’t work?”
